Design and Implementation of an Autonomous 2D LiDAR SLAM Robot with Distributed Microcontroller Telemetry and Real-Time ROS 2 Frontier Exploration
Mohit Sharma, et al.
Department of Robotics and Automation Engineering
Email: contact@slambot.io · Repository: github.com/Mohitsharma-2007/SLAMBOT
Abstract—Autonomous mobile robots operating in GPS-denied indoor environments require robust Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), deterministic motor control, and low-latency sensor telemetry. Traditional low-cost robotic platforms often suffer from microcontroller processing bottlenecks when performing concurrent LiDAR packet parsing, high-frequency encoder integration, closed-loop PID motor regulation, and SLAM computation. This paper presents the design, mathematical formulation, and experimental evaluation of SLAM Bot, an open, modular differential-drive autonomous mapping platform. The architecture decouples physical motion control from sensor acquisition through a heterogeneous dual-microcontroller layout: an Arduino Uno R4 WiFi (32-bit ARM Cortex-M4 @ 48 MHz) executing a 50 Hz deterministic PID velocity loop with 700 CPR quadrature encoder odometry, and a dedicated NodeMCU ESP8266 handling 115,200 baud UART streaming and real-time WebSocket serialization for a 360° Slamtec RPLIDAR A1M8 laser scanner at 5.5 Hz. High-level SLAM and autonomous navigation are orchestrated through a distributed ROS 2 Humble and Nav2 pipeline integrated with a FastAPI WebSocket relay and an interactive web dashboard. We implement an asynchronous 2D graph-based SLAM system (slam_toolbox) leveraging Ceres optimization, coupled with a BFS-based continuous frontier exploration algorithm for autonomous room mapping. Experimental validation demonstrates sub-centimeter mapping fidelity, stable 20 Hz odometry broadcast with under 2.1% rotational drift, and zero data packet loss across continuous exploration cycles.
Keywords—Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), ROS 2 Humble, Autonomous Exploration, Frontier Detection, Differential Drive, Dual-MCU Architecture, LiDAR Telemetry, Nav2, PID Control.

I. Introduction

Autonomous mobile robots have become ubiquitous across warehouse automation, environmental inspection, and search-and-rescue domains. A cornerstone requirement for autonomous ground vehicles is the capability to navigate through unknown environments without human teleoperation, known as Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM).

In low-cost research and educational robotics, designers frequently struggle with the limits of single-microcontroller boards. Handling high-baud serial streams from laser scanners while simultaneously counting microsecond-accurate quadrature encoder pulses and executing velocity PID loops causes severe interrupt starvation and serial packet dropping.

To overcome these bottlenecks, this paper proposes a decoupled dual-microcontroller architecture paired with a distributed ROS 2 Humble edge stack, achieving full autonomy, sub-centimeter mapping accuracy, and cloud/browser observability.

II. System Architecture & Co-Design

The platform employs a multi-tier decoupled topology, separating real-time deterministic hardware tasks from computational graph optimization:

1) Motion Controller (Arduino Uno R4 WiFi): Features a Renesas RA4M1 32-bit Cortex-M4 microcontroller running a dedicated 50 Hz closed-loop PID velocity controller.

2) Sensor Acquisition Node (NodeMCU ESP8266): Dedicated strictly to parsing continuous 115,200 baud binary packets from the Slamtec RPLIDAR A1 and streaming revolution arrays via WebSockets.

3) Computational Core (ROS 2 & FastAPI): Executes slam_toolbox, nav2 (SmacPlanner2D + DWB Controller), and a custom BFS frontier exploration node.

III. Differential-Drive Kinematics

Let $r = 21.5\text{ mm}$ be the wheel radius and $L = 150.0\text{ mm}$ be the track wheelbase. Given encoder resolution $N = 700\text{ CPR}$, the linear displacement per tick is:

$$\Delta s_i = \frac{2\pi r}{N} \cdot \Delta \text{ticks}_i$$

The incremental linear displacement $\Delta s$ and angular displacement $\Delta \theta$ over sampling period $\Delta t = 50\text{ ms}$ are computed as:

$$\Delta s = \frac{\Delta s_R + \Delta s_L}{2}, \quad \Delta \theta = \frac{\Delta s_R - \Delta s_L}{L}$$

The updated robot pose $[x_k, y_k, \theta_k]^T$ in the global odometry frame is estimated via Runge-Kutta 2nd order integration:

$$\theta_k = \theta_{k-1} + \Delta \theta$$ $$x_k = x_{k-1} + \Delta s \cos\left(\theta_{k-1} + \frac{\Delta \theta}{2}\right)$$ $$y_k = y_{k-1} + \Delta s \sin\left(\theta_{k-1} + \frac{\Delta \theta}{2}\right)$$

IV. Autonomous Frontier Exploration

To autonomously map the arena, our BFS frontier extraction algorithm evaluates the 2D occupancy grid map $\mathcal{M}(x,y) \in \{-1, 0, [1,100]\}$ generated by slam_toolbox:

1) Frontier Cell Identification: Every cell with $\mathcal{M}(x,y) = 0$ (free space) adjacent to at least one cell with $\mathcal{M}(x',y') = -1$ (unknown space) is marked as a frontier candidate.

2) Contiguous Clustering (BFS): Connected frontier cells are grouped, and centroids $\mathbf{c}_k = (\bar{x}_k, \bar{y}_k)$ are computed.

3) Cost & Safety Evaluation: Centroids within obstacle safety threshold $d_{\text{safe}} = 0.30\text{ m}$ are filtered out. The optimal target $\mathbf{c}^*$ is chosen by minimizing Euclidean travel cost:

$$\mathbf{c}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{c}_k} \|\mathbf{c}_k - \mathbf{p}_{\text{robot}}\|_2$$

VI. Conclusion & Future Work

We presented the design, implementation, and empirical validation of SLAM Bot, an autonomous differential-drive robot that resolves microcontroller resource competition through a decoupled dual-MCU architecture. Integrated with ROS 2 Humble graph SLAM and frontier exploration, the platform provides sub-centimeter mapping fidelity, deterministic low-latency control, and full web observability.

Future research will integrate 3D depth cameras and IMU Kalman filter sensor fusion (Extended Kalman Filter) to accommodate multi-floor terrain and non-planar obstacles.
